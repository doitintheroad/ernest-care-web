Partnership Liability Audit: Mitigating Duty-of-Care Exposure

Statutory Exposure Diagnostic

Under Ontario's WSIB Bill 127 and OHSA s. 32.0.1, employers carry an affirmative legal duty to mitigate chronic psychological injury resulting from sustained workplace demands. Providing a generic EAP that leadership refuses to use does not constitute a defensible duty-of-care posture. A clinical carve-out provides documented, proactive infrastructure.

The 5-Point Structural Risk Assessment

Point 01
Intake Velocity & Access Barrier Exposure

Risk: 3-to-4 week EAP intake queues result in unmanaged acute stress escalating into formal medical leaves.
Mitigation: Guaranteed 48-business-hour intake SLA on Core and Enterprise retainers.

Point 02
Privacy-Induced Underutilization Exposure

Risk: Senior partners avoid enterprise benefits out of fear that claims bleed into partnership reviews.
Mitigation: Complete statutory PHIPA isolation with direct portal access and blind billing.

Point 03
Provider Competence & Context Mismatch

Risk: Unqualified trainee networks give advice uncalibrated to high-stakes billable hour or board pressure.
Mitigation: Named Registered Psychotherapist with extensive professional services context expertise.

Point 04
Arbitrary Visit Caps (Token Allotment Failure)

Risk: 3-to-6 visit caps terminate care prematurely, stranding partners mid-treatment.
Mitigation: Dedicated 10-to-20 monthly session banks that flexibly absorb acute workload surges.

Point 05
Statutory Defensibility & Documentation

Risk: Inability to demonstrate proactive employer intervention during WSIB chronic stress claims.
Mitigation: Documented enterprise clinical retainer providing standing, accessible infrastructure.

Conclusion & Board Recommendation

Adopting an executive clinical carve-out closes the legal gap between corporate duty of care and practical benefit utilization. It protects the partnership's balance sheet, defends against WSIB claims, and preserves high-billing executive performance.
